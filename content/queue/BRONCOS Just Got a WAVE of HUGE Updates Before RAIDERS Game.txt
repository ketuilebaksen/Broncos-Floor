And here’s the crazy part. Everyone out there is asking why Sean Payton and George Paton haven’t pulled the trigger on a blockbuster trade. Everyone wants the headline. Everyone wants the splash. But I think a lot of people are completely missing what’s unfolding right in front of us.

Because when you connect the dots between the latest moves around the league and where Denver sits in the AFC right now, there’s a much bigger story here. A story about patience, about leverage, and about a team that doesn’t need to panic, with the Raiders waiting on the other side of this bye week.

So today, I’m going to walk you through exactly what’s happening, what the rest of the conference just did, and why I think Denver might already be winning this deadline without making a single headline move.

First, let’s set the scene, because context is everything here. We’re sitting at seven and two. We’re leading the AFC West. And we’re heading into our bye week riding five straight wins. Five in a row. That’s not luck. That’s not a soft schedule. That’s a football team that has figured out how to win.

Meanwhile, look around the league. Other teams are shipping away valuable draft picks like they’re running out of time. And honestly, some of them are. Some of these franchises are feeling the pressure of a closing window, an impatient fan base, or a front office that needs results right now.

And look, I get it. I understand why Broncos fans want Denver involved. You see another contender grab a recognizable name, the notification pops up on your phone, and immediately you wonder, why aren’t we doing that? Why are we sitting still while everyone else is loading up?

But here’s my question for you. What if keeping our options open is actually the smart move?

A team that’s already winning doesn’t have to act desperate. That’s the difference. Desperate teams overpay. Desperate teams make decisions out of fear. And with the deadline arriving today, I think that difference matters more than ever.

We are not chasing a lost season here. We’re not trying to save anybody’s job with one wild trade. We’re protecting a genuine chance to do something special. And protecting something special requires discipline, not panic.

Now, I’ve heard the complaints. Believe me, I’ve heard them. People saying George Paton is just watching the market and doing nothing. People questioning Sean Payton for not chasing every available star on the trade block.

But let me ask you something. Remember how much criticism Denver’s offseason moves received? Remember all the doubt? Some people were already questioning whether this roster was even good enough to compete. Some people had us finishing in the middle of the pack, maybe fighting for a wild card spot if things broke right.

Now look at where we are.

We survived a brutal stretch of games. We’re in first place. And we’re finding ways to win even when everything isn’t perfect. That last part is the most important. Good teams win when they play well. Great teams find a way to win when they don’t.

Sean Payton has never needed everybody outside the building to agree with his decisions. That’s never been how he operates. He knows exactly what kind of players fit his system. And George Paton knows how quickly a roster can get expensive when you start chasing names instead of fits.

Now, does that mean they should ignore a good offer if one comes along? Of course not. That’s not what I’m saying. What I’m saying is that I trust them to recognize a bad one. And at the trade deadline, recognizing a bad deal is just as valuable as finding a good one.

And Sunday’s comeback against Carolina told me everything I needed to know. This roster still believes in the plan, even under pressure. When things got tough, nobody pointed fingers. Nobody folded. They came back. That’s a locker room that trusts its leadership, and you don’t blow that up with a desperate trade.

Now, the perfect example of what I’m talking about is what Jacksonville just did.

The Jaguars went all in. They got aggressive. They added Jordan Davis from Philadelphia and Jessie Bates III from Atlanta. We’re talking about a first round pick and a second round pick going to the Eagles for Davis, and more high draft capital spent to bring in Bates.

On paper, those names sound scary. Jordan Davis is a massive presence in the middle of a defense. Jessie Bates has been one of the most respected safeties in the league for years. If you’re a Jaguars fan, you’re probably fired up right now.

But I keep coming back to one simple question. How much better did Jacksonville actually get?

Think about it. Their defensive line lost DaVon Hamilton to a season ending injury. Davis might help fill that gap, absolutely. But filling a hole isn’t necessarily the same thing as making a huge upgrade. You’re replacing what you lost. You’re not stacking talent on top of talent.

And yes, Jessie Bates is a major addition. No question about it. But remember, Jacksonville also lost Eric Murray to a season ending elbow injury. So Bates is coming in to fill that gap alongside Antonio Johnson. Again, this is partly about replacing what they lost, not simply adding another star to an already loaded group.

And they didn’t stop there. Jacksonville also acquired defensive tackle Davon Godchaux from New Orleans for a conditional 2028 sixth round pick. That tells you how seriously they’re taking those losses up front. They were scrambling to patch holes, and they paid a real price to do it.

Now here’s the part I really want you to remember. We already played Jacksonville this season. And we beat them 20 to 13.

They only put up 13 points against our defense, and that was even after building an early lead. So our defense has already seen this team, already figured them out once, and already shut them down when it mattered.

Now, I’m not going to sit here and pretend those new players won’t matter. They will. Bates can make plays on the ball. Davis can make running the football a real headache. Those are legitimate additions.

But the Jaguars didn’t suddenly become unbeatable just because they traded away a pile of picks. Their corners are still going to have to cover receivers. Their front still needs time to work together and build chemistry. New pieces don’t click overnight, especially in the middle of a season.

That’s exactly why I look at this move and feel better about Denver’s position, not worse.

And here’s something else. Jacksonville has more pressure on them now. When you spend that kind of draft capital, you’re telling everyone in the league, and everyone in your own fan base, that you expect results immediately. Not next year. Now. Jaguars fans are going to expect a serious playoff run. And if it doesn’t happen, those picks are gone, and the questions will get very loud.

That brings me to Howie Roseman, because if Denver ever gets on the phone with Philadelphia, I would think twice before rushing into anything.

Just look at how Roseman has handled trades over the years. Sam Bradford went to Minnesota. Carson Wentz went to Indianapolis. Haason Reddick went to the Jets. And this year, A.J. Brown went to New England for another big return.

Now, I’m not saying every one of those deals worked out perfectly for the Eagles. That’s not the point. The point is that Roseman has a habit of getting serious value whenever another team wants one of his players. He knows how to work the market. He knows when someone’s desperate.

It honestly reminds me of the reputation Danny Ainge built in basketball. Other front offices know that when they call him, they might be paying a premium. And Jacksonville just accepted that risk. They paid the Roseman tax.

Denver doesn’t have to copy that. Philadelphia now has future options and extra ammunition, while Jacksonville has to make its new players deliver right away. That’s the trade-off. And I’d rather be in Philadelphia’s shoes in that deal than Jacksonville’s.

And here’s why I feel so strongly about protecting our picks. Think about what our own draft picks have produced.

Pat Surtain II became one of the best cornerbacks in all of football. A true shutdown corner. A franchise cornerstone.

Bo Nix gave this franchise a quarterback to build around. After years of searching, years of frustration at that position, Denver finally found its guy through the draft.

Nik Bonitto developed into a dangerous pass rusher, the kind of player offensive coordinators have to game plan around every single week.

And then you’ve got Marvin Mims Jr., Troy Franklin, and Jonathan Cooper. Guys who remind me that value doesn’t end after the first round. Real contributors come from every round when you draft well and develop well.

So when someone says, just send a first and a second for another star, I want them to stop and remember what those picks can turn into. Would I trade the chance to find another Bonitto, or another Quinn Meinerz, without thinking carefully about it? Absolutely not.

Because that’s how you turn one exciting afternoon into a problem two years from now. That’s how you end up with an aging roster, no cap space, and no young talent coming through the pipeline. The draft is the lifeblood of a sustainable winner, and Denver knows that.

Now, I know the name everybody really wants to talk about. Jonathan Taylor.

Indianapolis has been willing to listen to offers, and the asking price sounds steep. Adam Schefter reported that Indianapolis was looking for a package similar to what Carolina received for Christian McCaffrey. And that package included second, third, fourth, and fifth round draft picks.

Let that sink in. That’s a serious investment in a running back. That’s a lot of draft capital for one position, and it’s a position that historically doesn’t age well.

Don’t get me wrong. Taylor is still talented. I would love to see what he could do in Sean Payton’s offense. The thought of him running behind this line, with Bo Nix working play action off of him, that’s exciting stuff.

But Taylor is approaching 28, and running backs take a beating. Every carry is a collision. Every season adds wear and tear. So if Denver gives away important picks, the deal has to improve this team for more than one Sunday. It has to be worth it over the long haul.

Now, New England has also come up in some of the speculation around Taylor. But the strongest trade reporting has focused on teams like Denver, Green Bay, Minnesota, and the Giants. So I’m not treating the Patriots as a confirmed serious bidder. But their running back situation is absolutely worth watching.

And here’s the thing about calling Indianapolis. Just making the call can teach Denver what the market looks like without forcing anyone into a trade. Information is valuable. Knowing the price helps you make smarter decisions, whether you buy or not.

That New England angle matters for another reason too. The Patriots already spent major draft capital bringing in A.J. Brown. And now their backfield has real questions.

TreVeyon Henderson has been away from the team while dealing with a serious health issue, and honestly, I hope he gets all the time and support he needs. Some things are bigger than football.

And Rhamondre Stevenson is now expected to miss the next few weeks with a plantaris tendon injury behind his knee, although surgery isn’t expected.

So I understand why people connect New England to Taylor. But if the Patriots pay another huge price, that’s another future asset they can’t use anywhere else. That’s another pick gone from a team that’s already spent big.

And let me be clear. I don’t want to celebrate anyone getting injured. That’s never what this is about. I just want our Broncos to avoid making a rushed decision because another contender is feeling pressure. Their panic doesn’t have to become our panic.

Besides, before we declare a running back emergency, let’s take an honest look at our own room.

R.J. Harvey is on injured reserve with a knee injury, but his earliest possible return is later this season. That’s very different from losing him for the year. He’s coming back. That matters.

J.K. Dobbins still gives Denver an experienced option, even if the production hasn’t always been what we wanted. He knows this offense, and experience counts for something in big games.

And Jonah Coleman has grown into a bigger role. He showed against Carolina that he can carry real responsibility. That’s a young player stepping up when the team needed him, and that kind of growth during a season is exactly what you want to see.

Remember how thin that running back room felt during our last playoff run? We were leaning so heavily on Harvey after injuries changed the entire plan. There was almost no margin for error.

Now, I don’t think this group is perfect. I’m not going to pretend it is. But I do think it has more answers than people are giving it credit for. And a healthy rotation could be worth more than one costly headline.

Of course, running back isn’t the only place Denver could improve. There’s another name I’ve been keeping an eye on, and this one is on defense.

Budda Baker.

Vance Joseph knows Arizona. He has strong connections there, and he understands exactly what kind of player Baker can be. A leader, a hitter, a guy who plays with his hair on fire every single snap.

Now, I’m not saying Denver has a deal worked out. Let me be very clear about that. This is a possibility I want to explore with you, not a report.

But think about the situation. Brandon Jones is in the final year of his contract and has been dealing with injuries. Talanoa Hufanga has missed time too. With two starters recently sidelined, I completely understand why safety help would catch the front office’s attention.

Imagine adding another experienced safety to a defense we’re already excited about. That could give Vance Joseph so much more flexibility. More disguises, more looks, more ways to confuse opposing quarterbacks. And it might help us handle injuries later in the season, when depth becomes everything.

But again, and I’ll keep saying it, the price has to make sense.

And since we’re talking about smart upgrades, I have one offensive line name to throw out there. Landon Dickerson.

I’ve watched stretches where Ben Powers misses a block, and every time, it gets me thinking about what another proven guard could bring to this line. Protection for Bo Nix is everything. Running lanes for Coleman are everything.

Dickerson has played at a Pro Bowl level, although injuries have interrupted his season. He’s back from injured reserve now, so the question isn’t only what he was before. It’s what he can give you right now, today, for this playoff push.

And remember, he’d be coming from Roseman’s Eagles. That’s not a conversation where I’d casually throw away picks. You go into that call with your eyes wide open.

Seattle’s move for Quenton Nelson is a great reminder of the risk. They landed a terrific guard, a big name, a proven player. But now he’s week to week after a knee procedure. He’s not on injured reserve, but it shows you that a big name never comes with zero risk. You can pay full price and still end up waiting.

So where does all of this leave us?

I think Sean Payton and George Paton should absolutely keep calling teams. Keep working the phones right up until the deadline. If there’s an affordable player who helps us at safety, on the offensive line, or anywhere else, go get him. I’m all for it.

And if the best move turns out to be handling contracts or investing in players already in Denver during the bye, that’s fine too. We have needs, sure. Every team does. But we aren’t trying to repair an entire roster in one afternoon. This isn’t a rebuild. This is a contender making adjustments.

Look, I’ve seen teams chase big names before, only to regret the draft picks they gave away. It happens every single year. Sometimes staying patient isn’t exciting. It doesn’t trend on social media. But it gives you more options when you actually need them.

The point isn’t that waiting always wins. It doesn’t. The point is that having a plan beats following the crowd. Every time.

Sean Payton has shown he can prepare this team for big opponents, including tough NFC teams. When the stakes are high, this coaching staff shows up with a plan.
