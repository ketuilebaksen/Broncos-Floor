Let’s start with the move Denver actually made. The Broncos acquired inside linebacker Daiyan Henley from the Los Angeles Chargers.

And honestly, I find this one really interesting. Because when you look at this roster, defense isn’t exactly the first place you’d think Denver needed help. This defense is already capable of making life miserable for opposing offenses. It’s been the backbone of this team. It’s been the reason we’ve won some of these close games.

So why add a linebacker?

Let’s look at what Henley brings. He brings athleticism. He brings tackling ability. And he brings experience in pass coverage, which is huge for a modern linebacker. In today’s NFL, if your linebacker can’t hang in coverage, offenses will find him and pick on him all day long. Henley gives you a player who can handle those assignments.

But here’s the part that really caught my eye. With the Chargers, Henley handled defensive communication responsibilities, including wearing the green dot. For those who don’t know, the green dot means you’re the player with the radio in your helmet. You’re getting the calls from the sideline and relaying them to the rest of the defense. You’re the quarterback of that unit.

And that matters more than people realize. When you’re playing close games, one missed assignment or one coverage breakdown can completely change the outcome. One guy lining up wrong, one blown communication, and suddenly a receiver is wide open for a touchdown. Having a linebacker who’s used to carrying that responsibility is a real asset.

So Henley gives defensive coordinator Vance Joseph another experienced option in the middle of the field. Another smart, athletic player he can trust.

But let’s pump the brakes a little. According to the reporting, Henley is expected to begin as the third inside linebacker behind Alex Singleton and Justin Strnad. So don’t assume he’s walking in and immediately taking somebody’s starting job. That’s not how this works.

He needs to learn the system. He needs to learn Vance Joseph’s terminology, his calls, his adjustments. And he needs to earn his opportunities on the practice field and in games.

But if he performs well? I wouldn’t be surprised at all to see his role grow as the season goes on. That’s the beauty of a move like this. It gives you options. It gives you flexibility. And it gives you insurance if anything happens to the guys ahead of him.

And personally, I love it from a competition standpoint. Competition makes a good defense better. Nobody gets comfortable. Everybody has to bring it every single day in practice, because there’s someone right behind them ready to take their snaps.

Henley’s arrival also puts some extra pressure on the younger linebackers, guys like Karene Reid and Jordan Turner. They now know there’s another experienced player in that room competing for playing time. And sometimes that’s exactly the push young players need.

The Broncos are trying to build a defense that can hold up when the games get even bigger. When it’s December. When it’s January. When every snap matters. Henley is part of that plan.

But bringing Henley onto the roster created an immediate problem.

Denver was already at the 53-man limit. And when you’re at the limit and you add a player, somebody has to go. No way around it.

And that somebody was defensive back Shaun Fresh Jr.

Now, Broncos country, this is the part of the story that really deserves your attention. Because Fresh wasn’t just some guy sitting at the end of the bench. He had been making real contributions.

Think about his journey. He arrived from the United Football League. Not exactly the typical path to an NFL roster. He worked his way onto Denver’s practice squad, kept grinding, kept proving himself, and eventually earned a spot on the active roster against the Carolina Panthers.

And when he got his chance, he delivered. Against Carolina, he returned two kickoffs for 62 yards, including a 41-yard return on the very opening play of the game. Think about that. First play of the game, and he’s giving his team great field position right out of the gate.

And that’s valuable. Especially when your offense is still searching for consistency. When your offense isn’t moving the ball easily, every extra yard of field position matters. Starting a drive at the 40 instead of the 20 can be the difference between points and a punt.

And it’s not like the coaching staff didn’t notice him. Sean Payton had previously praised Fresh for his intelligence, his quick change of direction, and his versatility as a nickel defender and a returner. That’s real praise from a head coach who doesn’t hand out compliments for no reason.

So let me be clear. This wasn’t a player who had nothing to offer. Denver made a calculated decision. A tough one.

And here’s the gamble. The Broncos reportedly hope Fresh clears waivers so they can bring him back to the practice squad. That would be the best case scenario. You keep Henley, and you keep Fresh in the building.

That’s the risk. And that’s why this decision has people talking.

Now, the Broncos also released Ugo Amadi from the practice squad, which could potentially create room for Fresh to return if he does clear waivers. So you can see the front office already planning ahead, trying to set up that scenario.

And there’s another reason Denver might feel comfortable making this move. The cornerback group could get a lot healthier after the bye week.

If those players are available coming out of the bye, Denver should have a lot more flexibility in the secondary. Suddenly, losing a depth defensive back doesn’t feel as painful when you get your star corner back and your other contributors healthy.

But until their availability is confirmed, there’s still some risk here. Injuries don’t always follow the timeline you want. A concussion can linger. An ankle can take longer than expected. And if those guys aren’t ready, Denver could find itself thin in the secondary at exactly the wrong time.

And that’s what makes this whole situation so interesting. The Broncos strengthened one position while potentially sacrificing depth at another. They took a calculated risk. Whether that decision works out depends on two things. How Henley performs, and how healthy the secondary becomes.

If Henley turns into a key piece of this defense and the corners come back healthy, this move looks brilliant. If Fresh gets claimed and the secondary gets hit with more injuries, people are going to look back at this moment and ask questions.

The Broncos have been dealing with injuries at running back. R.J. Harvey and Tyler Badie have been on injured reserve. Veteran J.K. Dobbins has struggled to deliver consistent production. The backfield has been a revolving door at times.

And with Bo Nix at quarterback, a reliable running game can make everything easier. It creates manageable third downs. Third and three is a whole lot easier than third and nine. It opens up opportunities through play action, because linebackers have to respect the run. And it prevents defenses from focusing entirely on the passing game and teeing off on your quarterback.

That’s exactly why a player like Taylor generated so much excitement. He’s the kind of back who changes how a defense has to play you.

But here’s the important distinction. The deadline has passed, and Jonathan Taylor was not traded to Denver.

Other running backs, including Breece Hall and Tony Pollard, were also discussed as possibilities. But those possibilities never became completed Broncos trades either.

Because here’s the truth. The defense can’t be expected to rescue the offense every single week. At some point, the offense has to carry its share of the load.

You don’t need a superstar at every position. No team has that. But you do need reliable options when opposing defenses take away your primary receivers. Sometimes that’s a tight end finding a soft spot over the middle. Sometimes it’s a running back leaking out underneath. And sometimes it’s simply a better play call that scheme someone open.

Denver has to find those answers with the players already in the building. There’s no cavalry coming.

Jaylen Waddle hasn’t delivered the production many people expected. Courtland Sutton remains an important part of this offense, but his age and contract situation raise some longer-term questions. This organization has to be judged by results.

The Broncos entered their bye week in a promising position. No doubt about it. But winning close games doesn’t mean every weakness has disappeared. Close wins can hide problems. And that’s why this break is so important.

But if the Broncos want to become a more complete football team, a real contender, the offense has to carry more of the load.

Broncos Just Pulled Off TWO Major Problems!

Guys, the Broncos just pulled off one of the smartest moves of the entire trade deadline. George Paton managed to get something really valuable without giving up much at all, and when you consider which team he made the deal with, it gets even better. At seven and two, that’s exactly the kind of move I want to see from this front office.

But here’s what bothers me. While everyone is celebrating what Denver just gained, I’m looking at what we still don’t have. And there are two major problems on this roster that could make this trade deadline look very different come January.

So today, I’m going to explain why this trade looks like an absolute steal, and why two major problems could still hold the Broncos back from where we all want this team to go.

What’s up, Broncos country, and welcome back to Broncos Floor. If you’re new here, hit that subscribe button, because we break down every move this team makes, the good, the bad, and everything in between.

Let’s get into it.

Look, even with the Chargers sitting at zero and eight, I honestly didn’t expect them to send one of their captains to Denver. Think about that. A division rival. A team that has to play us twice a year. And they hand us one of their leaders.

Meanwhile, we’re seven and two. We’re leading the AFC West. And we’re trying to build something serious this season.

Then, less than an hour before the deadline, Denver landed linebacker Daiyan Henley from Los Angeles. Less than an hour. That’s a front office working the phones right up until the final minutes.

And let that sink in for a second. That’s a team inside our own division helping the team sitting above it in the standings. These two teams hadn’t made a trade with each other in almost 40 years. Forty years! That tells you how unusual this deal really is.

When the deadline hit at four Eastern, that was Denver’s only trade. Just one. And I know some of you wanted more. But honestly, one addition can still be a really good one. And for me, it’s the price that makes this deal a steal.

Here’s what Denver gave up. A fourth round pick in 2027 and a fifth round pick in 2028. That’s it. Henley was originally a third round pick back in 2023, and now we’re getting a player with real starting experience for two later round selections. That’s good business.

And it gets even better. Albert Breer says Denver expects a fourth round compensatory pick in 2027 because John Franklin-Myers left in free agency. Now, to be fair, that isn’t guaranteed. And it doesn’t mean this trade literally costs nothing. But if that pick comes through, it could seriously soften the blow of giving up that fourth rounder.

Now compare that with what other teams paid at this deadline. San Francisco got DeForest Buckner for a fourth. Seattle paid a third and a fifth for Quenton Nelson. Jacksonville spent a second and a fifth on Jessie Bates III.

Different positions, sure. Different contracts, different situations. But the point is clear. Denver didn’t mortgage its future. The Broncos didn’t empty the cupboard. They got a quality player at a reasonable price.

And we’re not just buying a name here. Henley led the Chargers with 51 tackles through eight games this season. Back in 2024, he racked up 147 tackles. Over his career, he’s also added 12 tackles on special teams, five sacks, and four interceptions. This is a productive football player.

And just look at what he did against Houston on November 8th. Henley picked off a tipped pass from C.J. Stroud. I love how quickly he reacted on that play. The ball gets tipped, and boom, he’s right there. That’s instinct. That’s awareness. That’s the kind of play that changes a game.

But I also want to give you a fair warning here, because I’m not going to just hype this up without telling you the full picture.

PFF gave Henley a 71.3 coverage grade in 2024. That’s solid. But through eight games this season, he’s sitting at 38.3. That’s a big drop. So, in a way, Denver is betting that his best football is still in there, and that a new system and a winning environment can bring it back out.

He turns 27 this month, and his rookie contract expires after the season. So let’s be honest about what this is. This is a playoff audition, not a guaranteed long-term answer. If he plays well, Denver has a decision to make. If he doesn’t, the Broncos didn’t lose much.

Now, I know what some of you are thinking. Why spend picks on a linebacker when our defense is already winning us games?

Here’s my answer. Because good isn’t the same as deep.

Alex Singleton has been carrying a huge load inside. Justin Strnad is right there with him. Those two have been playing a lot of snaps. And over a long NFL season, a lot of snaps start to add up.

On the outside, Que Robinson helps the rotation. But we’ve already seen how quickly that group can get thin. Jonah Elliss went on injured reserve, and Dondrea Tillman showed up on the injury report with a hip issue. Injuries pile up fast in this league.

Inside, here’s what I want to see. Can we give Singleton or Strnad a breather and still cover a running back coming out of the backfield? Can we rotate without losing anything? That’s where Henley comes in.

And look at what this defense has already done. Denver allows about 20.7 points a game, which ranks 10th in football. We’ve got 26 sacks and eight interceptions. Zach Allen and Nik Bonitto have four and a half sacks each, and Malcolm Roach has four. That’s a defense that gets after the quarterback.

Adding a linebacker who has started 41 games, playing behind that kind of pressure? That makes a lot of sense to me.

And look at the cornerbacks, because that’s how this team has been built. Pat Surtain II sets the standard. He’s the best of the best. And then you’ve got Riley Moss, Jahdae Barron, Ja’Quan McMillian, and Kris Abrams-Draine, all giving Vance Joseph different answers and different looks.

Here’s an interesting detail. Denver promoted Shaun Fresh Jr. from the practice squad on November 7th, but then waived him just three days later while making room for Henley. To me, that tells you something. It tells you Denver values having its next man ready, and it’s constantly shuffling the bottom of the roster to make sure it’s as strong as possible.

And here’s why depth matters so much for this team in particular. Remember how one-score games hurt us back in 2024? We went one and six in those games. One and six! That’s painful.

This season, six of our seven wins have come by eight points or fewer. Six of seven. Every week is a nail-biter. And in those moments, one tired defender or one missed assignment can decide everything. Depth isn’t flashy. It won’t trend on social media. But it absolutely matters.

Still, I can’t pretend this was the entire shopping list.

Broncos Wire reported that Denver checked on running back Jonathan Taylor, tight end Trey McBride, and also looked at safeties. None of those conversations turned into another arrival.

Sean Payton said the team wasn’t looking to sell, and they certainly didn’t. But they also didn’t buy the offensive help that a lot of us expected.

Think about the history here. In 2024, Denver sent Baron Browning to Arizona for a sixth round pick. Last year, we stood pat, reached the AFC Championship Game, and fell just short. This time, Payton made one deal. But I still can’t ignore the two positions we didn’t address.

And that brings us to the two major problems.

Problem number one. Tight end. And honestly, for me, this is the most frustrating one.

Evan Engram has 17 catches for 169 yards and one touchdown on 30 targets through nine games. That’s not what I pictured. When Payton talked about making Engram a key part of this offense, I imagined a lot more than that. I imagined a matchup nightmare over the middle.

And here’s the thing. His cap charge is about 14 million dollars this year. At that price, expectations should be a lot higher.

Adam Trautman got a three-year, 17 million dollar extension, but he has just six catches for 55 yards. Nate Adkins has six catches for 44 yards, although, to his credit, three of those catches were touchdowns.

Put it all together, and that’s 29 catches and 268 yards from the three tight ends combined. Twenty-nine catches. That’s not enough.

And this isn’t a new problem. Last season, this position ranked 26th in the league with 709 receiving yards. So far, the new approach hasn’t fixed it.

Look, I don’t need a superstar at tight end every Sunday. I’m not asking for that. What I need is someone the quarterback can trust on third down. Especially when nobody’s getting open on the outside. That safety valve. That reliable target. Right now, Denver doesn’t consistently have it.

Problem number two. The backfield.

J.K. Dobbins leads the team with 324 rushing yards on 98 carries. That’s about 3.3 yards per attempt, with three touchdowns. We signed him for two years, and Payton has said the team looks at who’s got the hot hand.

Well, against Carolina, Dobbins carried the ball just four times for 18 yards. Meanwhile, rookie Jonah Coleman had 17 carries for 57 yards and a score. Coleman has 191 rushing yards overall, plus 13 catches for 128 yards, and he’s clearly earning more chances. The kid is growing into this offense right in front of us.

Then there’s R.J. Harvey, who went on injured reserve with a knee injury. Before that, he had 25 catches for 207 yards, which made him our most productive receiving back. The earliest he can return is the Miami game on December 6th.

And here’s an interesting number. Between Dobbins, Coleman, Harvey, and Tyler Badie, those four backs count about 10 million dollars against the cap. Engram alone counts for about 14 million.

So I can absolutely see why Denver checked on Taylor. Because right now, there’s one question this offense still can’t answer. Who gets the ball when we absolutely need four yards?

And those two problems, tight end and running back, explain why the bigger offensive numbers bother me so much.

Let me hit you with the numbers. Denver averages about 277 yards a game. That ranks 29th in the NFL. We score 22 points per game. We convert just 32.4 percent of our third downs, which ranks 31st. Thirty-first! We run for 91 yards a game at 3.7 yards per carry. And through the air, we’re putting up around 186 net yards per game.

Jaylen Waddle leads our receivers with 337 yards. Courtland Sutton has 266. And Pat Bryant has 89 after working his way back from an ankle injury. Nobody is taking over games week after week.

So how are we winning? One big reason is the turnover margin. We’re plus five. We take care of the football, and we take it away from the other team. And when the offense does get into the red zone, roughly 63 percent of those trips end in touchdowns. That’s efficient.

We survive because we don’t hand our opponents easy chances. But here’s the thing, Broncos country. Surviving a game and controlling a game are two very different things. And come playoff time, you want to control games.

Now, that brings me to Bo Nix. Because he’s the reason I can still truly believe in this team.

He’s thrown for 1,730 yards, 11 touchdowns, and only four interceptions, completing about 61 percent of his passes.

Now, I’ll admit, the early stretches of games can look rough. He threw for just 23 yards against the Chargers and 107 against Seattle. But you know what? Denver found a way to win both of those games.

And lately, he’s been heating up. Over the last three games against Arizona, Kansas City, and Carolina, he’s averaged 227 passing yards with five touchdowns and just one interception.

And his late-game calm? It’s been special. Truly special. His November record sits at eight wins and just two losses. And when Pat Surtain talked to Jordan Schultz, he made it clear just how much he trusts his quarterback. When your best defensive player believes in your quarterback like that, it means something.

Now, Payton keeps getting asked about slow starts, and I understand why. You can’t spend three quarters searching for rhythm every single Sunday. That’s not sustainable. We’ve become very good at close finishes, but eventually you want to put teams away early.

And before anyone says a team scoring 22 points a game can’t win a Super Bowl, let’s remember our own history. The 2015 Broncos scored 22.2 points per game. They finished 19th in scoring. And they won it all.

But here’s the difference. That defense allowed only 296 points the entire regular season. That was a historic unit. Our current defense is 10th in points allowed. It’s good. It’s not that.

Look at the last 11 champions. Nine of them finished in the top 10 in regular season scoring. The only exceptions were those 2015 Broncos and the 2023 Chiefs with Patrick Mahomes.

And Seattle, the latest champion, scored 483 regular season points and beat New England 29 to 13 in February.

Last year’s Broncos won 14 games and scored 401 points. But New England stopped us in the AFC title game. With Jarrett Stidham playing that day, we learned just how small the margin can be. One injury, one game, and the season is over.

So what can change without another trade? Actually, quite a bit.

For starters, Harvey returning would bring back an important receiving option underneath. Bryant is already back from injured reserve, and the bye should help him get comfortable again. Coleman can keep growing with more touches, while Dobbins stays fresher if the rotation is handled well.

On defense, Vance Joseph gets to figure out exactly where Henley fits behind Singleton and Strnad. And thanks to the bye, Henley has until November 22nd to learn the calls before we play the Raiders. That’s valuable time.

And coming out of the bye, the schedule gets intense. After the Raiders visit on Sunday, we’re at Pittsburgh on Friday, November 27th. Miami comes to Denver on December 6th, followed by the Jets and another Raiders game. Buffalo visits on Christmas. Then we head to New England before finishing the season against the Chargers.

Kansas City is still chasing us in the AFC West. And even Las Vegas gets two shots to mess with our position. We split with Kansas City. They got us in the opener, and we took the rematch 28 to 24.

So here’s what I’m watching coming out of the bye. Engram’s targets. How Payton divides the touches between Dobbins and Coleman. Whether Henley improves our coverage. And whether Bo Nix finally gets us moving early in games.

And there’s one more thing I’m watching. Bo’s career win total. He’s just six wins away from breaking Russell Wilson’s NFL record for the most regular season wins by a starting quarterback in his first three seasons. He’s at 31 right now. Five more wins gets him even with Wilson at 36. And the sixth would put him all alone at the top.

That makes these remaining games even more exciting.

I just don’t want the excitement to hide what happened on offense. Tight end still needs answers. Running back still needs a clear plan. Maybe our defense gets even stronger with Henley in the mix. But if this team wants to make a real run in January, the offense has to find another gear, and it has to find it with the players already in the building.
